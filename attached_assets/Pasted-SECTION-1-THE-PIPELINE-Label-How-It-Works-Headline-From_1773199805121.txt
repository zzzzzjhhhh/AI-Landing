SECTION 1 — THE PIPELINE
Label: How It Works
Headline:
From the physical world to a model-ready dataset — with nothing lost in translation.

Step 1 — Capture
Label: Real World. Real People. Real Scenarios.
We deploy trained annotators into the environments that matter — homes, workshops, industrial settings, and beyond. Every collection session is designed around the specific use cases, object categories, and interaction types our clients need their AI to understand.
This isn't screen recording. It's embodied data collection — first-person, multi-angle, environment-rich.

Step 2 — Annotate
Label: Nothing Goes Unnamed.
Every object, surface, action, and spatial relationship is labelled with precision. Our annotation protocols are purpose-built for robotics — capturing not just what is in a scene, but how things relate, how they move, and how a human navigates them.
We go beyond bounding boxes. We deliver structured intelligence.

Step 3 — Validate
Label: Quality That Compounds.
Every dataset undergoes rigorous multi-pass quality review before delivery. We track consistency across annotators, environments, and edge cases — because a single systematic error in training data becomes a systematic failure in the field.

Step 4 — Deliver
Label: Ready to Train On.
Datasets are delivered in model-ready formats, structured to client specification. Clean, consistent, documented — built to accelerate training cycles, not complicate them.

SECTION 2 — WHAT WE ANNOTATE
Label: Dataset Capabilities
Headline:
Rich data for the full complexity of physical space.
Content areas:

Object recognition & spatial mapping — 3D object identification, size, position, surface properties, and physical relationships within a scene
Manipulation & grasping — how humans pick up, move, and place objects; hand positioning, grip type, force signals
Environment diversity — kitchens, workshops, warehouses, public spaces, and custom environments on request
Edge cases & failure modes — cluttered scenes, poor lighting, ambiguous objects, interruptions and recovery actions
Human-robot comparative sequences — parallel recordings of humans and robots performing the same tasks, for alignment and fine-tuning
